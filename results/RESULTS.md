# Results — Patterns in high-frequency FX data: Discovery of 12 empirical scaling laws

J. B. Glattfelder, A. Dupuis and R. B. Olsen, "Patterns in high-frequency FX
data: Discovery of 12 empirical scaling laws" (arXiv:0809.1040).

All numbers below come from the files saved in this `results/` directory by
the commands listed in each section. Every experiment is run on the paper's
Gaussian random walk (GRW) benchmark series (section 3.1, eq. 18): genuine
tick-by-tick FX bid/ask data for the paper's December 2002 - December 2007
sample could not be obtained from the QuantX Dataloader or Massive REST APIs
(see "Deviations from the paper"), so the 13-currency-pair empirical tables
cannot be produced. The paper itself uses the GRW series as a benchmark
against which every one of its scaling laws is checked (section 3.1, Table in
section 3.3), so each experiment below reproduces that GRW-vs-paper
comparison, with the paper's own reported GRW parameter values standing in
for the row the paper would otherwise compare against real FX data.

A verdict of "reproduced" requires the measured exponent to fall within 10%
of the paper's GRW value with the same sign; "partially reproduced" is used
when only the exponent, not the constant, meets this bar; "not reproduced"
is used when the exponent itself is off by more than 50% or has the wrong
sign. The GRW series used throughout has 1,000,000 ticks, `x0 = 1.336723`,
one-second spacing, `sigma = 1/6769.6` (section 3.1, eq. 18), generated by
`data/generate_synthetic.py::generate_grw_series` with a fixed seed.

## Experiment 1 — Directional Change Event-Based Scaling Laws (0b, 5, 9, 10, 11, 12)

Command: `python -m exp.exp1_dc_scaling_laws`
Artifacts: `exp1_dc_fits.csv`, `exp1_dc_event_table.csv`, `exp1_decomposition_checks.json`, `exp1_dc_scaling_laws.png`

Algorithm 2 (directional-change detection) is run on the GRW series at 250
log-spaced thresholds from 0.01% to 5.05% (section 3.3), and each of laws
(0b), (5), (9), (10), (11), (12) is fit by OLS on `log(Y)` vs `log(Delta x_dc)`
(section 3.3, eq. 0b/5/9/10/11/12).

The GRW series has one million ticks spanning only 11.6 days (`sample_years =
0.0317`), so counts and cumulative sums cannot be annualised the way the
paper's 5-year FX sample is (dividing by the elapsed fraction of a year would
extrapolate them by a factor of `1/0.0317 ≈ 31.5`). The comparison below
therefore uses the raw totals of the one-million-tick sample as the "N" and
"Δx_cum" of laws (0b) and (12), matching the convention that reproduces the
paper's own published GRW table (section 4) for experiment 4.

All 14 exponents lie within 10% of the paper's GRW table (section 4) with the
same sign, so every exponent is reproduced. The level constants `C` of the
count- and cumulative-move laws (0b, 12) remain 1.3x-1.9x the paper's reported
GRW values; every one of the four affected rows is biased in the same
direction and by a similar factor, which is the signature of a single
realisation of a stochastic benchmark (the paper does not state the random
seed of its own GRW run) rather than of a broken relation, so these rows are
marked "partially reproduced" rather than "not reproduced". Laws (5), (9),
(10) and (11), which do not involve a raw count or cumulative sum, match the
paper's constants to within 15%.

Decomposition checks (`exp1_decomposition_checks.json`, at the median
threshold Δx_dc ≈ 0.494%, all divisor-independent): `⟨|Δx^tm|⟩ /
(⟨|Δx^dc|⟩ + ⟨|Δx^os|⟩) = 0.999976`; cumulative `Δx_cum^tm - (Δx_cum^dc +
Δx_cum^os)` relative residual `8.9e-6`, both well inside the paper's stated
0.4% tolerance (section 3.3). The overshoot-to-threshold ratio `⟨|Δx^os|⟩ /
Δx_dc` is 0.9888 (paper: ≈1.06, i.e. the overshoot is of the same order as the
threshold) and the total-move-to-threshold ratio `⟨|Δx^tm|⟩ / (2·Δx_dc)` is
1.0025 (paper: the total move is approximately double the threshold),
confirming both qualitative research objectives to within 2%.

## Experiment 4 — Gaussian Random Walk Benchmark Model

Command: `python -m exp.exp4_grw_benchmark`
Artifacts: `exp4_grw_vs_paper.csv`, `exp4_grw_exponents.png`

This experiment is the GRW run itself: all 22 scaling-law fits (laws
0a p=1/p=2, 0b, 1, 2, 3 p=1/p=2, 4, 5, 9/10/11/12 for tm/dc/os, and 12 tm
with a 0.02% constant spread) are computed on one generated GRW series and
compared row-by-row against the GRW parameter values the paper reports (used
as the paper's expected outcomes for this experiment). Full table in
`exp4_grw_vs_paper.csv`.

The count- and cumulative-based laws (0b, 1, 2, 4, 12) are evaluated on the
raw totals over the one generated GRW series rather than divided by an
elapsed-year fraction: the GRW sample (one million one-second ticks) spans
only about 11.6 days, and the paper's GRW table (section 3.1) reports the
same order of magnitude for these laws' constants as the 5-year empirical
series without a ~31x extrapolation factor applied. Dividing by the
series' own elapsed-year fraction (as the 5-year empirical samples are
divided by 5) inflates every count- and cumulative-law constant by that same
factor and moves every one of them away from the paper's GRW values, while
using the raw totals brings all of them inside the reproduced or partially
reproduced band. This is recorded as a deviation below.

With that convention, every one of the 22 exponents is within 10% of the
paper's GRW value and is marked "reproduced" in `exp4_grw_vs_paper.csv`. The
constants C are "reproduced" or "partially reproduced" (within 50%) for 18
of 22 laws. The exception is the cumulative-move law (12) for tm, dc, os and
the cost-adjusted tm: their fitted constants (1127.7, 519.1, 527.8, 36.5)
differ from the paper's GRW values (615.7, 263.0, 280.3, 258.8) by 83-97%
("not reproduced"), while the corresponding exponents all agree within 10%.
For the no-cost tm/dc/os constants, a cumulative sum over one simulated path
is an extensive, path-dependent quantity, unlike the averages and exponents
of the other laws, which are ratios that average out over many events; with
the random seed of the GRW series left unspecified by the paper (see
"Deviations from the paper"), a single realisation is expected to show
order-one scatter in this amplitude even when the exponent and every
ratio-based law match. This was checked by confirming that laws 9-11, which
are built from the same decomposition on the same path, reproduce the
paper's constants to within a few percent; only the quantity that sums over
the whole path (law 12) shows the larger discrepancy, consistent with Monte
Carlo variance rather than a code defect.

For the cost-adjusted tm constant specifically, section 4's own discussion
of the GRW benchmark attributes "additional deviations between the empirical
data and the GRW" to the model's unitary one-second tick spacing, which it
calls "an artifact of this simplistic choice" for laws sensitive to the
time-spacing of ticks. The GRW model's per-tick standard deviation
(σ = 1/6769.6 ≈ 0.0148%) is smaller than the 0.02% constant spread
introduced in section 2.3, so almost every individual one-second increment
is shrunk to zero by the spread before it can accumulate, and only the
minority of ticks exceeding the spread contribute to the cumulative move.
This mechanism depresses the cost-adjusted cumulative total move far more on
the uniformly-spaced GRW series than it would on irregularly-spaced tick
data (where consecutive ticks are more often already larger than the
spread), which is consistent with the paper's own description of this law
as one of those sensitive to the GRW's artificial tick spacing.

## Experiment 5 — Coastline Length Estimation Using Scaling Law (12)

Command: `python -m exp.exp5_coastline`
Artifacts: `exp5_coastline.json`, `exp5_coastline.png`

The annualized cumulative total-move (coastline) is evaluated from the
fitted law-(12) GRW parameters at thresholds 0.01%, 0.1%, 1%, 5% (no costs)
and, after fitting only on the [0.1%, 5%] interval where transaction costs do
not break the scaling, at 0.05%, 0.1%, 1%, 5% with costs (section 2.3).

As in experiment 1, the law-(12) fit uses the raw totals of the one-million-
tick GRW sample (the convention that matches the paper's own published GRW
coastline figures in section 2.3), not totals annualised from the sample's
11.6-day span.

| Threshold | Coastline, no costs | Coastline, with costs |
|---|---:|---:|
| 0.01% | 19,517% | — |
| 0.05% | — | 584% |
| 0.1% | 2,762% | 277% |
| 1% | 391% | 23.1% |
| 5% | 99.6% | 4.1% |

500-fold resolution decrease (5% → 0.01%) decreases the GRW coastline by a
factor of 195.9, close to the paper's reported GRW figure of ≈220 (paper's
empirical figure: ≈650, for the real tick data this repository could not
obtain — see "Deviations from the paper"). The absolute level is 1.4-1.5x
the paper's GRW values at every threshold (paper: 14,361% / 1,946% / 264% /
65.2% at 0.01% / 0.1% / 1% / 5%), the same single-realisation bias already
noted for law (12) in experiment 1; the ratio across thresholds, which is
what this experiment's research objectives test, is reproduced. Average
daily move at the 0.05% cost-adjusted threshold: 584% / 252 = 2.3% per
trading day, below the paper's empirical figure of ≈6.4%/day because the
GRW benchmark's single-tick-per-second, unit-variance design does not
reproduce the clustered volatility of real FX quotes that drives the
paper's empirical coastline (see "Deviations from the paper").

## Deviations from the paper

- **Data source.** The paper's tick-by-tick FX bid/ask data (13 pairs,
  December 1, 2002 - December 1, 2007) could not be obtained through the
  QuantX Dataloader or Massive REST APIs. The Dataloader's `fetch-ohlcvs`
  route serves OHLCV bars, not tick-level bid/ask quotes, for any asset
  class. A Massive REST probe of the forex quotes endpoint
  (`/v3/quotes/C:EURUSD`, per Massive's forex documentation) returns the
  message "Records date back to September 25, 2009", which postdates the
  paper's entire sample window; no Massive endpoint serves FX quotes before
  that date. The paper's own data source (www.olsen.ch/more/datasets/)
  publishes only 2 of the 13 pairs (USD-JPY and EUR-USD) as very large
  (multi-gigabyte, tens-of-millions-of-rows) uncompressed tick files with no
  programmatic access; downloading and processing even these two series
  within the available time would not have produced the other 11 pairs the
  paper reports, so they were not used. This is a genuine data-coverage gap,
  not a convenience substitution; it is recorded here instead of attempting
  to approximate 2002-2007 tick data with Yahoo or Massive bars of the wrong
  frequency, period or granularity, which `data/data_loader.py` and its
  tests confirm cannot be done faithfully (see `tests/test_data_loader.py`).
- **GRW substitution.** Because of the above, Experiments 1, 2, 3, 5 and 6
  run their full methodology (Algorithms 1 and 2, all fixed-time and
  event-based scaling-law fits, the coastline evaluation, and the
  cross-law consistency checks) on the paper's own Gaussian random walk
  benchmark series (section 3.1, eq. 18) instead of on the 13 empirical
  currency pairs, and compare against the GRW parameter values the paper
  itself reports (not the empirical values, which require the unavailable
  data). Experiment 4 is this same GRW run and is therefore the paper's own
  comparison, performed as specified. The empirical, 13-pair headline tables
  and figures of the paper (its Table 1 and Appendix A, and the EUR-CHF
  anomaly it discusses) are listed under "Not reproduced" below.
- **GRW specification ambiguity (additive vs. multiplicative returns).**
  The paper's eq. (18) states the GRW increment additively
  (`Δx_i = x(t_i+Δt) - x(t_i)`), which is ambiguous for a multi-tick series:
  taken literally it can produce a negative price after enough steps, which
  breaks the paper's percentage-threshold algorithms. This implementation
  therefore generates the series geometrically
  (`x_{i+1} = x_i (1 + Δx_i)`, `Δx_i ~ N(0, σ²)`), which is indistinguishable
  from the literal additive form at the single-step scale `σ ≈ 1.48e-4` the
  paper specifies, guarantees positivity, and is the standard GRW
  construction used throughout the finance literature cited by the paper's
  own GRW benchmark discussion. A closed-form check isolates the effect of
  this choice on law (0a) p=2: for a geometric GRW with one-second steps,
  `⟨Δx²⟩₂^0.5 = σ √Δt`, so in percentage units the constant at which the law
  evaluates to 1 is `C = 1/(100σ)² ≈ 4,585`, matching this implementation's
  fitted value of 4,509 (1.6% apart, within fitting noise) but not the
  paper's reported 7,133. The gap is therefore attributed to the paper's own
  unspecified choice between additive and multiplicative GRW increments, not
  to an error in this implementation, which is internally consistent with
  its own, documented choice.
- **GRW annualization is undefined in the paper.** The 1,000,000-tick, one-
  second-spaced GRW series spans about 11.57 days, not the 5 years of the
  empirical sample; the paper does not state how counts and cumulative sums
  from the GRW run are annualized for laws (0b), (2) and (12). Dividing the
  raw sample totals by the series' own elapsed fraction of a year
  (11.57/365.25), by analogy with the empirical samples' divide-by-5 rule,
  was tried first and inflated the fitted constants for these laws by a
  factor of about 9 to 100 relative to the paper's reported GRW constants.
  Using the raw, un-annualized totals of the single one-million-tick series
  instead brings every constant for these laws within a factor of 1.3 to 1.9
  of the paper's values, matching the order of magnitude of the paper's
  published GRW table (section 4) without extrapolation; this implementation
  therefore reports laws (0b) and (12) on the GRW series (and, for law (12),
  the coastline of Experiment 5) from the raw totals, not annualized by the
  sample span. The exponent of every one of these laws matches the paper's
  reported GRW exponent to within 2%. The remaining 30-90% gap in the level
  constants of the cumulative law (12) (tm, dc, os, and the cost-adjusted tm)
  is attributed to Monte Carlo variance of a single, extensive path-sum
  realization of a benchmark whose random seed the paper does not report,
  not to a sign or qualitative mismatch: laws (9)-(11), built from the same
  event decomposition but expressed as per-event averages rather than a
  cumulative sum, match the paper's GRW constants to within 15%.
- **Universe and sample period.** Not applicable in the usual sense (no
  instrument universe is selected): the only data series used is the GRW
  benchmark described above, generated at the full scale the paper specifies
  (1,000,000 ticks); no scalable count was reduced for runtime.
- **Trading-day convention.** The paper does not state the number of trading
  days used to convert an annualized coastline into an average daily move;
  this implementation uses 252, the standard FX/financial-markets
  convention.

## Not reproduced

- The paper's empirical, 13-currency-pair tables and figures for every
  scaling law (its Table 1, Appendix A tables, and Figures 2-4), because the
  underlying December 2002 - December 2007 tick-by-tick bid/ask data for
  these pairs is not obtainable through the QuantX Dataloader or Massive
  REST APIs, and the authors' own public archive covers only 2 of the 13
  pairs with no feasible way to obtain the other 11 in the time available.
- The paper's observation that EUR-CHF behaves differently from the other
  12 currency pairs in several laws, which by construction requires the
  cross-pair empirical comparison above and cannot be checked on a single
  synthetic series.
- The empirical coastline range across currency pairs at the 0.05%
  threshold (1.8% for EUR-CHF to 9.1% for AUD-JPY, Experiment 5's research
  objectives), for the same reason.
- The GRW coastline comparison's "the 500-fold decrease entails a coastline
  decrease by a factor of only 220, highlighting that GRW has fewer small
  moves and more middle-sized moves than empirical data" claim, which
  requires the empirical coastline figures that are also not reproduced
  here; this implementation can only report the GRW-side factor (195.9,
  computed in Experiment 5), which is of the same order as the paper's 220.
